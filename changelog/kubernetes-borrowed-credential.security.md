On the Controller, a Kubernetes cluster with no credential of its own now onboards only with a stored credential of the cluster's own organization, and never when the cluster is in no organization, is reached through a route, or pins an authority the credential does not carry: before, onboarding searched every organization's credentials and let the cluster's record choose the authority and route, so whoever could write inventory could read another organization's cluster facts, and with their own authority and route could receive its token. A kubeconfig import now puts the cluster in the organization its credential is stored in, and refuses to replace a cluster of another organization. A template's Kubernetes credential is likewise checked only against its own `ssl_ca_cert` (or the system's authorities), never the authority or server name the cluster's record pins. A cluster that relied on borrowing across organizations needs its own credential.
