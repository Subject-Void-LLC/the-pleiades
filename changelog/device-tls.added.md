A `generic_http` or `generic_grpc` device's TLS is set on its own record: `tls_ca_pem` pins the authority its certificate must chain to, `tls_server_name` names what it is checked against, `tls_min_version` can raise the floor to TLS 1.3, and `tls_client_certificate: true` presents the device's stored certificate for mutual TLS (a certificate and key, or a PKCS#12 bundle). Certificates are always verified. Nothing lowers TLS by default: an old device that needs TLS 1.0 or 1.1 needs `tls_allow_deprecated_versions: true`, one that needs 3DES, RC4 or RSA key exchange needs the separate `tls_allow_legacy_ciphers: true`, and a `generic_http` device that must send its credential to an `http://` API needs `http_allow_plaintext_credentials: true`. Each is reported as a `WARNING` on every onboarding and every run, the last one advising that the credential be rotated. A flag that allows nothing is refused, and `generic_grpc` refuses both TLS weakenings, since HTTP/2 forbids them. `http.request`'s device mode now refuses `validate_certs: false` outright: the device's `tls_ca_pem` is how to trust its certificate.
